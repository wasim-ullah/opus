Result page (fix 1): what I found and what I propose

I completed the men's sexual-health questionnaire with test answers (no purchase) and reviewed the result page. The page recommends one medication with a monthly or one-time choice and a "Proceed to checkout" button. I measured it at desktop width, where the card is only about 414px wide, so the phone layout should be very close; the exact phone view is confirmed after approval.

  • The checkout button is about 1,000px down a page about 2,500px tall. On a phone that is more than a screen of scrolling before the first tap, and the button does not stay in view.
  • Clarity (last 3 days): both iPhone sessions on the result page had no taps. In one the page was hidden after about a second and stayed open for nearly ten minutes; in the other it was hidden and shown again three times. People arrive, leave to think, and do not continue. Clarity masks the page, so this is indicative only.
  • The product is added to the cart as soon as the page loads (the cart showed 1 item). Worth checking that this does not confuse buyers or add a duplicate.
  • Small tap targets: the "View Cart" and "Checkout" buttons in the cart message are about 117 by 34px, under the usual 44px minimum.

What the week 1 baseline and tests show

  • Mobile is where the business is. About $1,960 of $2,310 in paid-order revenue (since 31 Aug) came from phones. Desktop has only 10 orders, so mobile versus desktop cannot be judged yet.
  • Most buyers arrive in an in-app browser. 632 sessions came through the Instagram app and 599 through the Facebook app in 30 days, against 227 on Mobile Safari. Those in-app browsers are known to handle card entry, autofill and tracking badly, and about two thirds of the site's JavaScript errors mention their internals. This is a hypothesis to test, not a confirmed cause.
  • Facebook/Instagram mobile orders are kept less often. 29 of 49 kept, against 15 of 21 direct and 11 of 14 from Google and email. They account for 11 of 14 failed payments and 9 of 10 refunds.
  • Birth control path, last 30 days: 98 started the mini survey, 50 reached the full questionnaire (51%) and 29 reached the result page. Clarity shows no sessions continuing from the result page to checkout, which is a tracking gap on mobile, not proof nobody checks out.
  • Two test orders went through with the WASIMTEST100 coupon: #104892 on desktop and #104893 from an iPhone via a Facebook-style link. Both are marked TEST; nothing to ship.
  • Tracking setup progress. The old Meta pixel plugin is off, and ad and analytics tags are removed from the questionnaire pages (checked live). The server-side tracking container is created and linked to Tag Manager, and svc.opusmeds.com is added in Stape, waiting for the DNS record.

Overall recommendations

1. Mobile first (about 9 in 10 orders)

  • Approve and ship the four mobile checkout changes above: a visible, sticky Place Order button with the refund line, 16px fields, the coupon link lower, and a "try another card" message.
  • Test checkout inside the Instagram and Facebook in-app browsers, not only Safari and Chrome, because over half of visits open there and the failed payments and refunds cluster in that traffic.
  • Get the refund reasons and Stripe decline codes for Facebook/Instagram orders. They decide whether the fix is on the payment side or in who the ads reach.
  • Fix Clarity so mobile sessions are followed past the result page, then review mobile recordings for taps, scrolling and where people stop.

2. Desktop (22% of sessions, 10 of 94 orders)

  • The checkout fixes help desktop too: the refund line beside Place Order, the coupon link moved lower, and a "your cart is empty" message in place of the Men/Women screen when the cart is empty (three sampled desktop sessions ended there).
  • Do not judge desktop yet. Ten orders is too few: 8 were kept, 2 failed, none refunded, and the average order is higher ($44 against $36 on mobile). Re-check once there are about 30 desktop orders.
  • Clean the desktop traffic before reading it. Many desktop sessions last about 1 second with no click and come from outside the US, which looks like bots or accidental clicks. Report desktop on real sessions only so it is not made to look weak.
  • The desktop test order (#104892) went through correctly, so there is no known desktop checkout fault.

3. Across both

  • Failed payments hit about 14% of all orders (9 of the 18 were insufficient funds). Recover them with the clearer error message first; a follow-up email needs your approval and must contain no health information.
  • Finish the tracking setup (DNS record, then Google Analytics through server-side tracking) so every later test can be read per device.
  • Keep conversion data sent to Meta to an order value with no identifiers, and only after legal and vendor agreements are confirmed.
  • Re-run the weekly baseline and the device table every week, and judge each change by the before and after on the same device.

Meta ads review (prepared 20 November 2026)

Sources: WooCommerce orders, Stripe payments, Meta Ads Manager exports (both accounts), Microsoft Clarity (90 days). Conversion = paid orders ÷ Meta link clicks. ROAS = WooCommerce paid-order revenue ÷ Meta spend.

What the data says

  • Spend scaled, but ROAS stayed far below break-even. August to October: spend +58%, paid orders +79%, ROAS 0.065 to 0.083. Break-even on a first order is 1.0.
  • Clicks are not the limit. Only 0.42% to 0.49% of link clicks end in a paid order, which is $576 to $650 of spend per order against an average order of about $41.
  • Sales are lost in the funnel and at payment. Result page to checkout fell from about 50% to 13% in the last 30 days; 23% to 32% of Stripe orders in Sep to Nov were never paid.
  • Meta had no result to optimise on. About $24,400 of spend (September to 21 October) shows no results in Ads Manager.
  • Repeat purchases are not in this data, so whether a $41 order can ever pay back $576 is unknown.

Testing plan: proof over time

There is no single fix for conversion, so each change is measured against a baseline before it is trusted. Traffic is the constraint: at about 0.44% conversion, a purchase-level A/B test needs roughly 14,500 visitors per version to detect a 50% improvement, which is about five months of the traffic seen in September and October. So the plan starts with before/after measurement and moves to A/B tests on the earlier funnel steps once traffic returns.

Sample sizes are rough estimates (95% confidence, 80% power) from the baselines above and will be recalculated once the week 1 baseline is in. Any testing tool must be kept off questionnaire answers and checkout details, and chosen only after the client approves it from a privacy point of view.

Limits of this data

  • Clarity recordings were not watched; the AI summary failed, so this uses the dashboard, funnels and heatmaps. Clarity offers fixed windows, so "earlier 60 days" is 90-day totals minus 30-day totals.
  • Meta attributes on a 7-day click and 1-day view window, so orders can land in a different month from the click.
  • Monthly site visitors are not available; link clicks stand in for traffic. WooCommerce is the source of truth for orders and revenue.
